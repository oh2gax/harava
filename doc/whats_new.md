# What's new in Harava

Short notes for users, newest first. Each version is one day of development (v.DDMMYYYY).
The full technical history is in CHANGELOG.md. The newest heading gives the version shown
under the app name.

## v.12112026 — What really travelled with you, and what is always with you

- **At your stops, or only on the same road** — *Travelled with me* used to mark every address heard 500 m apart, which on a motorway takes 18 seconds. Harava now finds your **stops** (the phone stayed within 150 m for five minutes or more) and gives two levels. **at N stops** (amber): the device was heard at two or more of your stops, at least 500 m apart, so it was with you at both. **same road** (grey): it was beside you for at least two minutes while you moved a kilometre or more, but never at two stops, which is normal in traffic. Anything you only passed gets no tag. On a 23 km drive the old rule marked 57 addresses; now it is the watch or phone that rode along (a few rows) and some twenty devices on the same road.
- **Where and when** — the Selected radio panel has a *Travelled* row: at which of your stops the device was heard (by their times), or for how long and how far it shared the road. The *Travelled* count shows the devices at your stops, with the same-road ones beside it. The CSV export has the new columns `travel`, `travel_stops`, `travel_km` and `travel_min`.
- **One Android phone or watch is one row** — an Android device changes its address about every nine minutes, so one watch used to look like dozens of radios. Its Google advertisement stays the same over several address changes, and one form of it carries a code that stays for about a day. Harava now joins those addresses (*N addr.* tag; the panel says *same Google advertisement* or *Google day code*). In four days of data 1,100 addresses folded into 300 devices. Google does not document these advertisements; the day code is what the data showed, and the rule's note says so.
- **A device is judged as a whole** — travel is now decided for the device, not for each address: a watch that was at home under one address and at the destination under another was at both stops.
- **Twenty scooters are twenty scooters** — radios with the same maker's service and the same name were joined into one device. That is right for earbuds, which keep their name while their address changes, and wrong for a fleet of rental scooters that are all named "Scooter1". Only addresses of the changing kind are joined now.
- **Always with me** — a watch or phone without a name cannot be marked as yours by name, and its address changes every few minutes. When such a device travelled with you and was very close all the way (−55 dBm or stronger, at two stops or for ten minutes), its panel says so, and *＋ My device* saves a new kind of rule: **Always with me, on layer**. It marks everything on that layer that behaves like this, on every day. Mind what it cannot tell: a passenger's phone of the same kind, or a device left in your bag, is hidden as yours too. Switch *Hide my devices* off to see them.
- **The Sit joins in** — a Sit export tells only when a radio was first and last heard. Harava now puts both moments on your phone's own track, so a device the Sit heard at the start and at the end of a drive counts as travelled. The log holds about a tenth of what the Sit hears.
- **A short walk** — a walk of a few hundred metres has no two stops and no kilometre of road. A Bluetooth device that stayed close (−80 dBm or stronger) for five minutes over 250 m of your way now gets *same road*, and its panel reads *Beside you for 17 min while you moved 300 m*.
- **26 new rules from the road** — Ensto thermostats and EV chargers, other charge points, OBD adapters, Teltonika trackers, tachographs, rental scooters, Polestar and Volvo keys, Casambi lights, printers, air conditioners, CPAP machines, pumps, clocks and more. In four days of data the Unidentified Bluetooth radios fell from 285 to 190.
- **An iBeacon is not an Apple device** — the iBeacon format starts with Apple's company number whoever made the beacon; some cars send it. Such radios now read *iBeacon (beacon of any maker)*, on the Beacon layer unless Fieldwatch knows better (a Tesla stays a Vehicle).
- **Nothing to read** — a radio that sends only its flags or its power now says *No data in the advertisement* where it used to look like an unknown device waiting for a rule. It stays Unidentified.
- **Small corrections** — the Bluetooth adapter of a Mitsubishi air conditioner is no longer a vehicle; long values in the radio panel wrap at words.
- **Guides** — User guide 1.7 and Mobile guide 1.6 explain the stops, the two levels and *Always with me*; the figures show the new tags.

## v.10112026 — AirTags that are really iPhones, and Fieldwatch 1.1.43's new raw data

- **An "AirTag" is usually an iPhone** — Fieldwatch's *Apple AirTags* signature matches every Apple Find My message, and iPhones, iPads, Macs and AirPods send those too. Harava now reads the kind of device from the message: *Apple device (Find My)* moves to the Apple device layer and *AirPods or Beats (Find My)* to Audio; only *Apple AirTag* and *Find My accessory or newer AirTag* stay on the Tracker layer. In the first three days of data, 320 of 352 "AirTags" were Apple's own devices.
- **Sit-only radios** — a Sit export carries no advertisement, so the kind cannot be read: such radios show as *Apple Find My device, kind not known* and stay on the Tracker layer. Run the log together with the Sit.
- **Fieldwatch 1.1.43** — since 9 November Fieldwatch writes the whole advertisement into the log, where it used to write only the manufacturer data. Harava now reads both forms; before, many radios logged by the new version got a wrong identification (iPhones shown as AirPods), and Ruuvi temperatures were missing.
- **Keeping up with Fieldwatch** — Harava's signature table is now Fieldwatch's catalog 103 (version 1.1.43). The Identification page tells which catalog the table is from, and warns in amber when your data has signature names the table does not know: then Fieldwatch on the phone is newer than Harava. `python tools/update_fieldwatch_signatures.py` refreshes the table.
- **Two-way radio layer** — Fieldwatch 1.1.43 recognises the Icom ID-52 and the Bluetooth speaker-microphones of Icom, Yaesu and Kenwood; they get their own layer under Places & things.
- **Home & IoT split into more layers** — TVs, EV charge points, printers and sensors filled the Home & IoT layer. They now have their own layers under Places & things: *TV & media* (Samsung and LG TVs, Roku, soundbars, and a TV's own Wi-Fi Direct network), *EV charger*, *Printer* (HP, Epson, AirPrint, and printers' Wi-Fi Direct networks) and *Sensor* (Ruuvi, SensorPush and the like). Home & IoT keeps the lamps, smart-home gadgets and appliances. A charge point that does not tell its name is now recognised by the form of its device ID.
- **Phone / PC split by maker** — the biggest layer is now four: *Apple device* (iPhones, iPads, Macs), *Android / Google*, *Samsung* and *Windows PC*. Phone / PC keeps what is left, such as phone hotspots. The Phone check counts phones as before.
- **Safer imports and exports** — a code audit found that a specially prepared export file could run script in the page of whoever looked at that import, and that a radio name starting with `=` went into the CSV export as a spreadsheet formula. Both are closed; they needed a signed-in user to upload such a file.
- **Upload limit 100 MB** — one upload can now be at most 100 MB (it was 512 MB), and the page tells so before it sends anything. Fieldwatch's whole log is about 12 MB, so real exports fit many times over; the limit keeps a huge file from using up the server's memory. Admins can change it with `max_upload_mb` in `config.ini`.
- **Automated tests** — Harava now has tests that check the importers, the identification and the layers in a second. Put a fresh Fieldwatch export in `data/` and run `python -m unittest discover -s tests`: if a new Fieldwatch version has changed its log format, the tests tell before the results go wrong.
- **Guides** — User guide 1.5 and Mobile guide 1.4 explain the Find My kinds; the User guide also tells how to keep up with Fieldwatch. Admin guide 1.4 has the upload limit.

## v.09112026 — Import without duplicates and everyone's own data, the Harava phone app and the Workbench, Harava on the web with users and permissions, its own identification database, user guides, device layers, my own devices and Sit exports

- **Bluetooth paused** — when the screen goes dark, many phones (Samsung above all) park the Bluetooth scan while Wi-Fi goes on. The timeline now shades those stretches in amber, and *▨ Bluetooth paused* in its title tells how much of the day it was: Bluetooth radios there were not heard, so an empty stretch does not mean nothing was there. For walks and Sits, keep Fieldwatch in front with *Keep screen on*.
- **Database snapshot** — on the Maintenance page, admins make a complete copy of the data for analysis elsewhere and download it as one zip: the database, the Workbench's rules and a short info file. *My data only* leaves everyone else's data out; *Everyone's data* keeps all. The server keeps the 10 newest.
- **Share → Harava** — install the Harava phone app from Chrome (⋮ → *Install app*) and it appears in Android's share menu: in Fieldwatch, export the log or Sit, tap *Share* and choose *Harava*. The file is imported at once (or after choosing the phone, if you have several) and tells what it brought. Share the whole log whenever you like: only new rows are added.
- **Import everything, no duplicates** — Fieldwatch's log export always contains everything still on the phone, so exports made on different days repeat each other. Harava now keeps every hear only once per phone: drop all your exports at once (or a .zip, or *Scan folder*), and each file adds only what is new.
- **What each file brought** — the Imports page tells for every file: *new*, *extends* (a newer export of the rolling log, with how many new rows), *overlaps*, *duplicate* (nothing new) or *sit update* (a newer export of the same running Sit, which updates its radios instead of counting them twice). Deleting a file keeps the rows another file also has.
- **Coverage** — every import as a bar over time on the Imports page (log, Sit, KML, switched off); click one to analyse exactly its days.
- **Several days at once** — *Until* under *Day* makes a range of days: the map, the timeline, the lists and the counts cover all of them. ◀ ▶ move the whole range.
- **Your own data** — every import belongs to the user who imported it and others do not see it, unless you *Share* it with all users or with chosen users. *Whose data* chooses what you look at: your data and what is shared with you, one user's shared data, or for admins everyone's. My devices are personal too.
- **Phones** — choose which phone recorded the files you import. Two phones hearing the same radio stay two observations, and the radio gets a **2 phones** tag.
- **Re-read** — Harava keeps a compressed copy of every uploaded export, so a file can be read again when a new version reads more from it.
- **Your data moves along** — the first start converts the database by itself (all or nothing); your earlier imports, phone and My devices become the first admin's.
- **Harava phone app** — open `/app` (for example *harava.oh2gax.org/app*) on your phone and add it to the home screen: Harava in the same style as the WSPR Logger phone app. One view at a time fills the screen — *Map*, *Day*, *Watch list*, *Devices* (tap one for its own page), *Phone check*, *Identification*, *Imports* (upload exports straight from the phone) and, for admins, *Workbench*. Everything else is in the ☰ menu: the day, the views, each view's options, the theme, What's new and the guides. Back on the phone closes the menu or goes to the previous view; after a new version on the server the app reloads itself.
- **Workbench (admins)** — *🔬 Workbench* in the menu (`/workbench`, and in the phone app) lists every Bluetooth radio of all days that Harava cannot name yet, grouped by what it advertises: *Unknown*, *Weakly identified* and *No data*. One row is one kind of device to find out: open it for its advertisement decoded, the bytes that change, the days and places, what else was heard with it, hints and search links. Keep a status (New, Investigating, Identified, Ignore, Can't identify) and a note.
- **Make a rule** — when you know what a radio is, the Workbench suggests a rule from what its radios share; *Preview* tells what else it would catch, *Save rule* starts using it at once. Rules made here are kept on that server only (`ids/local_rules.json`, not in git) and come before the shared database; *Export rules and notes* downloads them, so good ones can join the shared database.
- **Phone app user guide** — *User guides* has a third guide, the Harava Mobile app User Guide.
- **Harava on the web** — Harava can now run on a server behind nginx with HTTPS, for example at *harava.oh2gax.org*, and you upload your Fieldwatch exports on the Imports page from anywhere. On your own computer it still opens without a sign-in, as before.
- **Sign-in** — once a user exists, every page asks for a username and password. *Keep me signed in* remembers you on that device for 30 days; ⏻ in the menu signs you out. After five wrong passwords the address has to wait 15 minutes.
- **Users and permissions** — every user has one permission: *View only* (look at the map, lists, Phone check and Identification, export CSV), *Analyze* (also upload and import exports, edit My devices and reload the identification database) or *Admin* (also manage users). Buttons you cannot use are not shown.
- **Maintenance page** — admins find *⚙️ Maintenance* in the menu (`/maintenance`): every user with first and last name, permission, an *active* switch and the last sign-in; *Add user*, *New password* and *Remove*; and a log of sign-ins, failed sign-ins and changes. A new user gets a one-time password and chooses their own at the first sign-in.
- **My account** — 👤 your name in the menu opens your account, where you change your password.
- **Mask addresses** — a switch under *Map layers* hides the second half of every address, all Wi-Fi network names and other people's device names on the screen, for screenshots you share. The data is not changed.
- **Admin and server guide** — *User guides* has a second guide: installing Harava on a server next to other sites, users and permissions, updating with `update.sh`, backups and troubleshooting.
- **Updates on the server** — `bash update.sh` updates Harava from GitHub in the same safe way as WSPR Logger: a database copy first, a check of the new files, then a restart and a test; `--rollback` goes back.
- **Harava identification database** — Harava now has its own database of Bluetooth patterns (`ids/harava_ids.json`). It names radios that Fieldwatch leaves *Unidentified*: Bluetooth Mesh lights and plugs, Apple AirPods, iPhones and Macs, Samsung Galaxy phones, Windows PCs, Philips Hue lamps, TVs, Oral-B toothbrushes and more. On 8 November it named 1,725 of the 1,782 Bluetooth radios that had no signature (97 %).
- **Identified as and Company / vendor** — two new columns on the Devices page and two new rows in the Selected radio panel. *Identified as* tells what the radio most likely is; a grey name with **?** means a weaker guess. *Company* comes from the official Bluetooth SIG list.
- **Group same device** — many devices change their Bluetooth address every few minutes. When the data shows that several addresses belong to one device, *Group same device* (on by default, in Filter) shows them as one row with an **N addr.** tag. Harava joins addresses only on proof: the same Bluetooth Mesh device number, or the same custom service *and* the same name. On 8 November 957 changing addresses folded into 23 devices.
- **EV charging points** — Bluetooth Mesh devices named *Charge* that wait to be set up are now *EV charging point (DEFA?)*. On 8 November 22 of them were heard around home, most only on the way through the parking area. A nameless address of such a device takes the name from its other adverts.
- **Identified as selector** — a new list under the search box shows only one kind of identified device, e.g. *Philips Hue lamp* or *Samsung TV*. It is grouped by device layer, and the numbers count what the other filters leave. Search also finds the *Identified as* and *Company* texts, e.g. `hue`.
- **Identification page** — *🔎 Identification* in the menu shows how much of your data is identified, which rules matched, and the most common patterns that are still unknown. *Reload database* reads the rule files again after you edit them.
- **Bluetooth SIG numbers** — the official lists of Bluetooth company and service numbers come with Harava (`ids/bt_assigned_numbers.json`, 4,065 companies). `python tools/update_bt_numbers.py` downloads the newest lists.
- **Harava** — the analyzer has a name: *harava* is Finnish for *rake*, and it rakes through what your phone heard. Start it with `start_harava.bat`. Your data moves from `fieldwatch.db` to `harava.db` by itself the first time.
- **One project folder** — Harava now lives in its own project folder, ready for git: put your Fieldwatch exports into `data\` (*Scan folder* reads it). `data\`, `tmp_files\`, the database and `api_keys.py` stay out of git.
- **What's new, User guides and About** — *✨ What's new* under the name at the top left opens one window with three tabs. The badge shows the version and turns amber when there is something new to read.
- **A user guide** — *User guides* has the Harava User Guide (PDF). It covers how to collect with Fieldwatch (log and Sit), how to import, every part of the map and the Devices list, the device layers, My devices and the Phone check, and what the data can and cannot tell.
- **Sit exports** — Harava now reads Fieldwatch *Sit export* files (CSV, JSON lines and KML). A Sit keeps every radio it heard, while the log writes at most 16 rows per scan.
- **Heard only in Sit** — radios that only the Sit has get a **sit only** tag, and the *Heard only in Sit* switch lists exactly what the log missed. A newer export of the same sit replaces the older one, and radios marked *Mine* in Fieldwatch become My devices.
- **Device layers** — every kind of radio in the Fieldwatch catalog is its own map layer with a switch. Click a name to show only that layer; click it again to show all.
- **Icon pins** — glasses, trackers, surveillance, police, drones and hacking tools get icon pins on the map. A tracker that is separated from its owner pulses red.
- **Glasses & VR** — smart glasses and VR headsets share one layer, because Meta uses the same Bluetooth ID for both. A name like *Quest 3* adds a **VR?** tag.
- **My devices** — mark your own router, PC, phone or sensors with **＋** (on a Devices row or in the map's detail panel). They are hidden from the list, the map and the Phone check while *Hide my devices* is on. Bluetooth devices change their address, so match them by name.
- **Graph for one kind of device** — the *Show* selector in the "Hears per 10 min" graph shows, for example, only Glasses & VR.
- **Map without watermark** — the CARTO key from `api_keys.py` (as in WSPR Logger) removes the "API key required" text from the map.

## v.08112026 — The first version: import, map, devices and the classroom phone check

- **Import** — drop Fieldwatch log CSV, JSON lines or KML files on the Imports page, or press *Scan folder*. A file already imported is skipped.
- **KML that Google Earth refuses** — Fieldwatch's own KML files, which Google Earth refuses because of hidden Wi-Fi names, load fine. A KML of the same time as a log is switched off, so radios are not counted twice.
- **Map** — the phone's path and one pin per radio, at the place where it was heard loudest. Bluetooth pins are filled and Wi-Fi pins are rings; a bigger pin is louder. Click a pin for its details, a signal chart and every place it was heard.
- **Timeline** — the graph above the map shows the hears and the new radios every 10 minutes; click a bar to zoom in.
- **Devices** — a sortable table of every radio with search, filters and CSV export. Tags show radios that **travelled** with you, trackers that are **separated** from their owner, and Ruuvi temperatures.
- **Phone check** — a classroom check: are all the phones in the box next to the teacher's phone? It counts phones in the box, in the room and outside, and *Auto-calibrate* sets the limit.
- **Everything stays here** — the data stays in a database on this computer. Leaflet and Chart.js come with the app, so only the map background needs the internet.
